## 1. What a two-dimensional topological theory describes

- **Space is a ring, spacetime a surface.** Objects of Bord_2 are disjoint
  unions of circles; morphisms are surfaces; Bord_2 is generated by the disk
  η : ∅ → S¹, the pants μ : S¹ ⊔ S¹ → S¹, the copants Δ : S¹ → S¹ ⊔ S¹ and
  the reversed disk ε : S¹ → ∅, subject to unit, associativity, commutativity
  and the Frobenius relation, all of which are diffeomorphisms of glued
  surfaces [CR Thm 3.5, 3.6; T6]. A 2d theory is a commutative Frobenius
  algebra on Z(S¹), internal to the target [CR Rem 3.9; T7].
- **The ring is self-conjugate.** Reflection is an orientation-preserving
  diffeomorphism S¹ → S̄¹, so its mapping cylinder is an invertible bordism
  and Z(S̄¹) ≅ Z(S¹) by an allowed reversible process. The point of Bord_1 has
  no such identification. This is the origin of every self-duality statement
  below.
- **The four processes.** Birth of a ring from the vacuum in a definite state
  (η); absorption of a ring (ε); merging of two rings into one (μ); splitting
  of one ring into two (Δ). The relations say these are the only moves and
  that surface topology fixes their algebra.
- **Gapped phases in one spatial dimension.** Z(S¹) is the low-energy state
  space on a periodic chain. A unitary 2d theory is a commutative
  C*-Frobenius algebra, hence semisimple, hence the functions on a finite set
  X of labels with a weight θ_x per label [DJ; Sawin Prop 1, 2, Thm 2; MS
  §3.1; T12–T16]. The labels are the degenerate vacua on the ring; merging
  compares labels (ε_x ε_y = δ_xy ε_x, superselection), splitting copies them
  (Δ(ε_x) ∝ ε_x ⊗ ε_x), Z(T²) = |X|, Z(Σ_g) = Σ_x θ_x^{1−g}. Kapustin–Turzillo's
  "D = 1" TQFTs are these with a symmetry [T24]. Standard reading; the filed
  sources give the algebra, not the condensed-matter gloss.
- **The classical structure.** Orthonormal bases are exactly the commutative
  dagger-Frobenius monoids: copy the basis, delete it [CPV Thm 5.1; T17]. So a
  unitary 2d theory *is* a set of perfectly distinguishable, copyable,
  comparable labels with weights. That is what surface topology forces.
- **Laboratory relevance.** Degenerate vacua on a ring and the fusion and
  fission of ring-like sectors are the 1+1d hallmarks; weaker than the 1d
  hallmarks (degeneracy on closed manifolds, pair production) and to be stated
  more cautiously. DECISION: how much condensed-matter framing to carry.

## 2. Generic facts from the unitary 2d TFT literature (the basis of the calculations)

- A unitary 2d TFT in FHilb is a commutative †-Frobenius algebra on Z(S¹),
  equivalently an orthogonal basis with weights; it is semisimple, a direct sum
  of one-point theories, and Z(Σ_g) = Σ_i θ_i^{1−g} [Sawin Prop 1, Thm 2, Cor 1;
  DJ §3; CPV Thm 5.1; T12–T17].
- A semisimple commutative algebra over a field is a product of field
  extensions; over R the only ones are R and C, so a semisimple commutative
  real Frobenius algebra is R^k ⊕ C^m (Wedderburn). Nilpotent summands are
  allowed by surface topology and are what unitarity excludes [Sawin Prop 2].
- Real forms. The d-point classical structure on C^d with an antilinear
  involution permuting the basis restricts to R^k ⊕ C^m on the fixed real
  subspace, k the fixed basis vectors, m the swapped pairs, k + 2m = d.
- **The plan (Pedro, 2026-09-07).** Every GPT has an ambient real vector space
  V. Its complexification carries the unique (up to basis and weights) unitary
  2d TFT; the GPT is a real form of it; what remains to show is that, restricted
  to the cone of states, the structure maps preserve the cone. The R^k ⊕ C^m
  decomposition is the central result. This makes the calculation close to the
  Vec case with structure added, and it is done for quantum theory in
  `results-quantum-2dUTFT.md`.
