## 1. What a one-dimensional topological theory describes

A gapped system, viewed at distances long compared with its correlation length,
has no metric left to see. What remains is a functor Z from the bordism category
Bord_n, whose objects are spatial slices, whose morphisms are the spacetime
regions between them, whose composition is gluing and whose product is disjoint
union, to the theory's own category of systems and processes. We take this
standard premise [Atiyah; Segal; Freed §1, T23] and ask what it demands of the
probabilistic theory in the target.

The demand is already visible in dimension one. Fix a slice E. Crossing with E
embeds Bord_1 into Bord_n: the point becomes E, its orientation reverse becomes
Ē, the interval becomes the cylinder E × [0,1], the two bends become the
cylinder read with both ends outgoing or both ends incoming, and the circle
becomes E × S¹. Every n-dimensional theory therefore contains a one-dimensional
theory for each of its slices. Bord_1 is generated by exactly these pieces,
subject only to the rule that two bends glue to a straight cylinder [CR Thm
3.4, T5]. Applying Z to that rule shows that Z(E) is dualizable, with dual Z(Ē)
and duality data the two bent cylinders [CR Lemma 2.4(a), Prop 2.6, T3]. This
is not a technicality. It is where the physics of a topological phase lives.

(i) *Closed spacetimes.* Every closed one-manifold is a union of circles, and
each circle is a bend glued to a bend. Z(E × S¹) = dim Z(E) is the size of the
low-energy state space on a closed spatial manifold: the ground-state
degeneracy by which topological order is recognised [T5, T8].

(ii) *Cutting and gluing.* The value of a region is recovered from the region
cut along a slice by contracting with the dual pairing of that slice, the sum
over intermediate states [CR Lemma 2.4(b), T2]. This is the topological form
of locality of action: a region is computable from its pieces because dynamics
propagates each piece to its causal development [A6, premise (ii)].

(iii) *Creation and annihilation in pairs.* The bent cylinder with both ends
outgoing is a state of the pair (E, Ē) fixed by topology alone: the pair
created from the vacuum. The opposite bend annihilates it. The rule that two
bends cancel says that annihilating one member against an incoming system
returns that system. For a point-like excitation this is the creation and
annihilation of the excitation together with its reverse; inside a phase it is
the bending of a line excitation, the rigidity of its fusion rules [T21, T26;
the anyon statement is standard but unfiled].

All three are laboratory notions: degeneracies on closed geometries, the
assembly of a system from its parts, the pair production and fusion of
excitations. And all three are the same diagram.

Now let the target be a general probabilistic theory. Dualizability of a
system X = (V, V^+, u) means three things [R1, R2, C7]. The reversed slice is
the dual system X^∨ = (V^*, (V^+)^*, u^∨), whose states are the effects of X.
The pair state and the annihilation effect are one and the same element
Σ_a f_a ⊗ f^a of V ⊗ V^*, which must lie in the theory's composite cone as a
state and in its dual as an effect. And every closed diagram is a morphism from
the trivial system to itself, that is, a nonnegative number. Written out, the
rule that two bends cancel reads: share the pair state between two parties;
let one party apply the annihilation effect to her half and to an incoming
system; the other party now holds the incoming system. This is teleportation,
exact and deterministic, with resource and measurement the same object [BBLW
Thm 1, C8], and a chain of such steps is entanglement swapping [DG Eq. 2,
C13]. The protocol that appears in the information-theoretic literature as a
striking consequence of entanglement is here the condition that a worldline may
turn around, which is the condition that a probabilistic theory can sit on a
spacetime at all.

The consequence is a criterion. A probabilistic theory whose systems cannot be
paired in this way admits no topological field theory in any dimension. It has
no closed-spacetime partition functions, hence no ground-state degeneracy on
any closed manifold. It has no consistent cutting and gluing, hence no locality
of action in the topological limit. It has no coherent pair creation, hence no
excitations with reverses, and its entanglement swapping degrades instead of
propagating. Such a theory can have arbitrarily strong correlations and still
be unable to describe a single topological phase or a single pair-production
event. Section 2 gives the criterion an operational form, every closed
worldline carrying one of the theory's own operations must have nonnegative
weight, and Section 3 shows that boxworld and the regular polygon theories
fail it.

## 2. The loop-weight criterion (stub)

**Theorem A (to state and prove).** In (GPT, ⊗) with a coherent composite
rule, X is dualizable iff the element Σ_a f_a ⊗ f^a is a state of X ⊗ X^∨ and
an effect on X^∨ ⊗ X for that rule; the dual is forced to be X^∨ [R1, R2].

**Theorem B (agent's proposal 2026-09-07, to check).** If X is dualizable and
the theory is tomographically local, then tr_V(g) ≥ 0 for every allowed
process g : X → X. Proof: the closed worldline carrying g is
cap ∘ σ ∘ (g ⊗ id) ∘ cup ∈ End(1) = R_{≥0}; the categorical trace is
independent of the duality data and equals the linear trace once the forgetful
functor to Vec preserves duals. Physical reading: a vacuum bubble carrying an
operation is a probability weight.

**Theorem C (sufficiency, sketch).** Take the composite cone of X and X^∨ to be
cone(T), the Choi states of the allowed transformations T. The cup is the Choi
state of the identity; cap positivity is exactly trace positivity of T. Owed:
coherence across multi-leg composites; the classical simplex as the worked
sanity check [G19].

## 3. Boxworld and the polygons cannot bend a worldline (stub)

The gbit's double bit flip (x, y, u) ↦ (−x, −y, u) is an allowed relabelling
[B §VI] with trace −1 on R³: by Theorem B no composite rule makes the gbit
dualizable. Composites of gbits inherit the flip on one factor. Regular n-gons
with n ≥ 4 and rotations allowed contain a rotation with 1 + 2cos θ < 0. Two
readings of the same obstruction: with ⊗_max the effects on a pair are
separable, so there is no annihilation [Short–Barrett 0909.2601, to file]; and
CHSH decays under iterated swapping [DLG Lemma 3]. Boxworld has the reversed
system (the rotated square) and the correlated pairs (PR boxes); it lacks an
annihilation compatible with its own operations. Recast the examples table of
`gpt-examples.md` as "which theories can bend a worldline".

## 4. Which theories pass (stub; quantum theory enters here)

Classical: stochastic maps have nonnegative trace; the perfectly correlated
pair is the cup. Complex quantum theory: Herm_N = H ⊗ H̄, tr(Ad_U) = |tr U|²,
CP maps have trace Σ|tr K_i|², composite the Choi (PSD) cone, the snake is
Choi–Jamiołkowski [SSC Ex 2.37]. Real quantum theory: the rotation by π/2 has
trace −1 on Sym², so it fails under tomographic locality; its actual composite
has a global sector (Y ⊗ Y) and the doubled character (tr O)² is nonnegative:
the global sector rescues it (OP4, R11). Balls with full SO(n) under
tomographic locality: only n = 1 and n = 3 pass (character 2 + 2cos θ for
n = 3). Owed: the rebit computation with the Sym₄ composite; the quaternionic
bit against BGW's exclusion [C12]; the relation to Dmello–Gross's χ_φ ≥ 0
[DG Thm 11].

## 5. Two dimensions and unitarity (stub)
