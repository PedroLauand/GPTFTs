# Title of the idea

*Everything below fills in over time, in any order. Leaving a section empty is
fine; it shows where the next contribution goes. You don't need a complete
thought before you write something.*

## The paragraph

*Required to enter. Roughly what you would tell a student to advertise the
question: what it is, in plain language, in one paragraph.*

## The problem

*A simple statement of what we're trying to solve. "We currently can't do Y",
or "we want to do Y".*

## Why bother

*Why the problem matters, or why we care about solving it.*

## Current approaches, and why we expect them to fail

*Approaches that might be applied to this problem, even if they weren't
designed for it. "Fail" means we expect them not to work for this particular
problem, and says nothing about the approach in general.*

## If we had a solution, what would it look like

*Not a proposed solution. A map of what kind of thing a solution would be: a
theorem of roughly this form, a construction with these properties, a no-go
result, a worked example.*

## How would we know we'd solved it

*What we would have to be able to show or state.*

## Uncertainties

*What has to be true for this to work? What can't be true? The aim is to
identify what would invalidate the idea, so we can check it early.*

## Sketch of a plan

*A sketch rather than a plan. Real detail for the first step or two only. The
first step is often filing and reading the relevant sources.*

## Open questions and disagreements

*Unresolved argument goes here rather than being smoothed away above. A live
disagreement usually means the idea is being worked on rather than stalled.*

## Judgement

*These questions are the filter, and they are for Pedro to answer rather than
the agent.*

**What would change if this worked?**

**Is the upper bound high enough?**

**What would make us less uncertain?**

## Sources

*Papers, notes, discussions and draft beats this draws on. Link into
`sources/` and cite beat labels rather than restating.*
